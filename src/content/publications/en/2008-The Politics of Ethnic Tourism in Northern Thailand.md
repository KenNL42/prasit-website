Much of the discussion about ethnic tourism in Northern Thailand has concentrated on the impact of tourism on ethnic communities, the interaction between tourists and villagers, and its conunoditization. This paper approaches ethnic tourism differently, examining instead the political relationship between tourists, middlemen (businessmen and the state), and ethnic tourees (the people the tourists come to see). Viewed through this lens, it can be shown that the interaction between the three ethnic tourism stakeholders is only superficial. Tourists who eagerly desire to explore unspoiled cultures and consume authentic experiencesland souvenirs tend to experience only the fake and dramatic ones, since there is only limited time for the encounter. The main purpose of the middlemen. and ethnic tourees for dealing with tourists is business only. Meanwhile the state, which is comprised of different agencies, plays contradicting roles in ethnic tourism and related ethnic minority issues. Based on four decades of experience of ethnic tourism in the highlands of northern Thailand, the more tourism develops, the more the ethnic culture loses its authenticity. Instead, the encounter becomes a staged event only performed for the tourist. Highland ethnic villagers, as tourees, have gradually learned and adapted to take advantage of tourism. Their encounters with other stakeholders in the industry are still as inferiors. Nonetheless, the recent introduction of alternative forms of ethnic tourism, including ecotourism and community-based tourism, has created more equality between the three parties. This paper draws from personal observations and interviews as well as secondary' sources.
