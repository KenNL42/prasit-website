Despite a growing social inclusiveness of global policy texts, conservation practice has not yet fully 25 embraced the meaningful participation of local and Indigenous communities residing in protected ar- 26 eas. In this context, the Thai government approved two Royal Decrees for ecosystem conservation in 27 November 2024 that would severely threaten the livelihoods, living prospects, and continued residence 28 of Indigenous communities in protected areas. Protests with 10,000 participants prompted a review of 29 the Decrees in May 2025. In response, this research report investigated the psychological and social 30 implications of the announcement of the Royal Decrees and argues that it undermined community 31 social capital and intrinsic engagement with everyday environmental heritage. We drew on qualitative 32 and survey data, collected from 09/2024 to 05/2025 in two Pgaz K’Nyau and two Hmong communities 33 in the northern Thai highlands, and comprising community consultations with 73 participants, 101 34 semi-structured interviews, and two survey rounds with 384 observations in total. We used qualitative 35 thematic and descriptive statistical analysis to explore how community members experienced, inter- 36 preted, and responded to the announcement of the decrees. We found that Indigenous villagers expe- 37 rienced anxiety and unease, and that the decrees undermined community efforts to conserve and en- 38 gage with local ecosystems. The continued uncertainty surrounding communities’ future sparked a 39 strong activist sentiment to resist the decrees. Disruptions of community social capital and everyday 40 environmental heritage also had plausible and potentially significantly negative ecological down- 41 stream effects.
