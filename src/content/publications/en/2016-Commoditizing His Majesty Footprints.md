This chapter investigates the ways His Majesty the King's visits to highland ethnic communities have been commoditized by the Royal Project and other relevant sectors. It is argued that the monarchy plays essential roles in launching projects relating to the contemporary popular tourism in highland ethnic communities. Importantly His Majesty's visits and initiatives which have become history and symbol have been commoditized for tourism purposes. The historical development of the monarchy and tourism in highland communities began from the time of Chao Dararassamee, the consort of King Rama V when she returned to Chiang Mai since the 1910s. From 1961 on, since the Phuphing Palace was built, His Majesty the King and family members have made visits to highland villages near the palace and elsewhere in the north. In addition to His Majesty's initiative on the Royal Project for introducing cash crops to replace opium cultivation among highland farmers in 1969, he donated funds and launched a store for local villagers to sell handicrafts for tourists. After promoting agricultural products for a few decades, the Royal Project started the tourism business in specific Royal Project sites in the early 2000s, with the slogan of "Following His Majesty's Footprints". Despite the fact that tourism has become popular in these Royal Project sites, highland ethnic peoples still do not participate much. Furthermore, tourists do not pay much attention to what His Majesty the King initiated but place more emphasis on exposure to nature, for leisure and relaxation.
