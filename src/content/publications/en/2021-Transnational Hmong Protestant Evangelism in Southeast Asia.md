The Hmong are a subgroup of Miao scattered in southwestern China and the upper Mainland Southeast Asia countries of Vietnam, Laos, Thailand and Burma.' After the Communists took over Laos in 1975, tens of thousands of Hmong refugees from Laos fled the country and finally settled down in Western countries including the United States of America, Canada, France, Germany, Australia, Argentina and French Guiana. The Hmong were formerly known as 'mountain people' in southwestern China and upper Mainland Southeast Asia. Since the colonial period, Christianity from Western countries has been spread among the highland ethnic minorities of upper Mainland Southeast Asia, replacing traditional beliefs (Hefner 1993: 3-7). Christianity as brought by foreign missionaries was one of the various 'civilizing' projects to which the Hmong have been subject during their history (Harrell 1995: 4-8). In order to attract new Hmong converts, some pioneer missionaries, such as Otto Scheuzger (1961: 30) have interpreted Yaw Sau, the Hmong God, as equivalent to the God of the Christians. In recent decades, Socialist governments have been concerned about a Messianic movement among some Hmong Christian groups in this region, especially in terms of rumours about the coming of a Messiah. In late April to early May 2011 there was a popular Messianic movement among Hmong Christians in northwestern Vietnam, particularly in the Dien Bien region. Even though the majority of Hmong people lived in remote areas and were strictly controlled by state authorities, through various means of communication they received news about a predicted Judgement Day proclaimed by eighty-nine-year-old Harold Camping, a Californian multi-millionaire who runs one of the largest Christian radio networks in the world.
